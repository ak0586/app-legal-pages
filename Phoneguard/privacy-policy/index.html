3. Android System Permissions & Prominent Disclosures

In adherence to both Google Play Developer Policies and the DPDP Act, PhoneGuard displays prominent in-app disclosure dialogs before requesting sensitive device capabilities:

  • Device Administrator Permission: Required strictly for Remote Screen Lock (to protect personal photos and banking apps from thieves) and Anti-Theft Uninstall Defense (preventing thieves from uninstalling PhoneGuard to kill tracking).
  • Background Location Permission: Required so you can locate your phone 24/7 if lost, even when the application is not actively open.
  • Camera Permission: Required strictly to capture evidence photographs when an unauthorized person repeatedly fails to unlock your phone.
  • Notification Listener & Default SMS Support: Required to intercept emergency trigger keywords sent via RCS or SMS without delay.

5. Data Retention & Storage Limitation Policy (Section 8(7))

Under the DPDP Act principle of Storage Limitation, personal data is retained only as long as necessary to satisfy the purpose for which it was collected:

  • Active Account Data: Your profile settings, trusted numbers, and current SIM configuration are retained while your PhoneGuard account remains active.
  • Intrusion Photos & Logs: Intruder photos and activity logs are stored in your encrypted cloud container. You may delete individual photos or logs at any time from the app or dashboard.
  • Account Deletion Purge: When you delete your account, all personal data—including profile info, trusted numbers, GPS logs, intruder photos, and SIM/ICCID records—is permanently and irreversibly wiped from our database immediately.

6. Protection of Children’s Personal Data (Section 9)

PhoneGuard is designed as an anti-theft tool for device owners. In strict compliance with Section 9 of the DPDP Act:

  • We do not engage in behavioral tracking, profiling, or targeted advertising directed at children.
  • We do not process any data that could cause detrimental effects on the well-being of a child.

7. Statutory Rights of the Data Principal (Sections 11, 12 & 14)

Under Chapter III of the DPDP Act, 2023, you are endowed with enforceable statutory rights regarding your digital personal data:

  1. Right to Access Information (Section 11): You have the right to obtain a summary of your personal data processed by us and the list of third-party processors with whom it has been shared.
  2. Right to Correction & Erasure (Section 12): You have the right to correct inaccurate data, update incomplete records, and request the complete erasure of your personal data.
  3. Right to Withdraw Consent (Section 6(4)): You may withdraw your consent at any time as easily as it was given, by toggling off permissions, turning off specific features, or deleting your account.
  4. Right of Grievance Redressal (Section 13): You have the right to register a grievance with our designated Grievance Officer regarding any matter relating to your personal data.
  5. Right to Nominate (Section 14): You have the statutory right to designate another individual who shall, in the event of your death or incapacity, exercise your rights as a Data Principal under the DPDP Act.
